## Background

Public Mechanics starts from one premise: **repair and delivery are design practices.** Systems don’t succeed or fail in theory — they are held together in practice by people, processes, and workarounds. This framework exists to make that work visible, legible, and transferable.

To do that, it draws from a wide range of traditions, but always through the lens of how systems actually deliver:

* **Understanding systems**: political economy, anthropology, urban studies, systems theory
* **Observing delivery**: service design, operations management, resilience engineering, HCI
* **Naming repair and care**: maintenance studies, labor research, feminist and critical design

The goal isn’t to create a closed canon or a new orthodoxy. It’s to give practitioners a common language for recognizing the work that keeps things running — and tools for building capacity around it.
